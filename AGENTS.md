## Learned User Preferences

- Ban telegram style first, ahead of every other writing instruction. Notes, briefings, screenplay and storyboard prose, skill text, and any organized explanation must stay fluent, readable sentences. If another instruction demands short sentences, terse lines, or compression, and the only way to obey it is telegram style—sentence fragments, slogan strings, or subjectless phrase lists—ignore that brevity demand and write complete sentences instead.
- Knowledge should enter and leave this vault through AI agents; stored notes still need meaningful structure and graph links, not an unstructured dump. The user also edits through the Obsidian Copilot plugin, so Cursor and Copilot writes must stay consistent.
- Notes, briefings, and any organized explanation to the user should sound like a mentor: fluent, readable prose with complete sentences; no telegram-style fragments, keyword dumps, or generic AI cadence. Lead with judgment, then reasons; mark tradeoffs and failure modes without lecture. Do not merely restate source or vault notes—extract the underlying mechanism. When a brevity or short-sentence rule would force telegram style, the ban above wins.
- Prefer discuss-then-ingest: explore and challenge an idea first, distill books deeply in chat when useful, and write to the vault only after the user explicitly agrees to save (e.g. 落库)—do not ingest on the first “read and distill” request alone.
- When walking through a named book, stay inside that source: teach the core arguments and what is worth applying thoroughly enough that the user can work from the briefing as if they had read it. Treat the user as an experienced market participant who wants sharper judgment, not as a beginner. Do not pad with outside training knowledge unless asked to compare.
- When absorbing books or other sources, treat them as `source` notes plus atomic `concept` notes (short excerpts and processed understanding), not a wholesale paste of the original. For several books in one task, give each its own source arc and terminology boundaries; corroborate existing concepts only when the mechanism matches, not when labels merely sound alike. Heavily illustrated craft books (e.g. storyboards): extract mechanisms from prose and read plates when layout carries the method—do not treat figures as skippable decoration.
- Vault output is Chinese regardless of source language. Do not think in English metaphors and then dress them in Chinese (法庭, 把边界留在明处, 拆掉优势, 升格). If a Chinese reader can understand the sentence but finds it awkward, rewrite. Explain as you would to an experienced Chinese market participant at the table, then tighten into prose; recast chat metaphors at ingest instead of copying them into notes. For English sources, understand first, then write with Chinese grammar, rhythm, and reading habits—not sentence-level translation. Keep English only for terms, necessary quotations, or names without a stable Chinese rendering.
- Keep the vault scalable across books from multiple domains; avoid per-book knowledge silos. When a later book covers a similar idea, always落盘 the new angle—deepen the existing concept with multi-source corroboration if the mechanism matches, or create a linked concept if the mechanism differs; never skip because something “similar” already exists. Cross-era, cross-genre agreement raises a concept’s reference value.
- Route video prompts by model. Seedance / 即梦 / 火山方舟 follow official Doubao Seedance 2.5 rules via the `seedance-prompt` skill; do not invent a prompt format. Thirty seconds is the per-shot ceiling, not the default length: set each shot by what the scene must do, keep openings short, and only let the peak approach 30 seconds; longer films are segmented or extended. Characters, locations, and hero props must be identity-locked across shots (stable appearance + reference binding; no face/wardrobe/set/prop swaps), given proper names, and those names must appear naturally in the scene—called, printed, or on a screen—not only as prompt IDs. Reverse and top views of the same room bind from a master pad image, not from prose that rebuilds the space. Inner states are visible micro-expressions; with a reference, do not redescribe face or clothes. Speech longer than one reaction cuts to the listener or a prop. No music must ban melody and pads, not only 「无 BGM」. In a frontal shot a phone shows only its back; cut to an over-shoulder or shoulder view to read the screen. Never put interface content on the phone back, and never show a face and screen text in the same frame. Canonical guide: https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/2607689?lang=zh
- MiniMax H3 paste text uses 图N for people/places/props in sentences; screenplay character and scene names and P-/L-/A- codes belong only in the **素材** upload row (and human **分镜对照**), not in **可贴入**—except quoted dialogue and on-screen/prop text. Storyboard **D#** codes are per-project camera registration for the shot index and crew cross-reference only—not industry-standard notation and not for H3 paste; spell 景别, 机高, focal length in millimeters, motion, and 图N instead.
MiniMax H3 / H3 Max / 海螺 / Hailuo / MiniMax Design prompts follow the `minimax-h3-prompt` skill: 4–15 second integer clips, product formula (duration + ratio + 图N binding + subject/scene/motion/sound), three modes (text, first/last frame, full reference). Do not reuse the Seedance timestamp template. H3 has no cross-clip memory: each clip must be a standalone paste, with identity, setting, style, bans, voice, and music written into that clip; do not rely on a shared preamble. Do not preprocess a join between clips: do not carry in the tail of a previous line, a leftover word, or any sound that belongs to another shot, and do not point ahead to the next shot. The paste describes only what this shot presents. Invoke people and props as 图N in the sentence, not names only on the image, and do not replace a bound person with 他, 她, 他们, or 她们. An offscreen voice that has no reference image repeats 画外的男人 or 画外的声音. Quoted dialogue and on-screen text stay verbatim. Reverse and top views of the same room bind from a master pad image. Inner states are visible micro-expressions; with a reference, do not redescribe face or clothes. Note natural blinking on performers when useful; do not mandate fixed-interval blink counts. Speech longer than one reaction cuts to the listener or a prop. No music must ban melody and pads, not only 「无 BGM」. When converting a storyboard, each clip states an exact focal length in millimeters (no zoom) and this clip's color and light only—do not paste full-film palette rules, scene-number cross-references, or other screenplay-background boilerplate into H3 paste text. On trading-chart clips, candlesticks are 绿涨红跌 with real OHLC construction (body between open and close, wicks to high/low, next bar opens at the previous close). When the chart or a UI control must be readable, use a short over-shoulder then a hard cut into that surface—not a slow push of the whole monitor; face and readable button or chart text must not share a frame. Dialogue and voiceover use normal speaking speed with at most one stressed word, and state 语速、语调、语气; instructions like 放慢 or 停半拍 make the line drag. Storyboard shorthand stays in the human 分镜对照 and is translated into a process the model can perform: write 画面左侧/右侧 or 从画面左边缘伸进画来 (left/right as the viewer faces the frame). 画左/画右/画下 get swapped or read as the actor's own hands. Do not paste 眼睛落在 or 眼睛先落在. That wording is the viewer's first landing, so write it as focus inside the shot size already chosen for this clip, and say to keep that size with no cut to a close-up and no push-in. If this clip is already a close-up, an insert, or a hand detail, say to hold that size and not push closer. The performer's own gaze is written as 图N的视线. Unfinished actions stop on the held frame (指尖靠近，在碰到皮肤之前停住). Those bans are only the last paragraph inside 可贴入, opened with 不要做, so copying 可贴入 includes them. Do not also emit an external **不要做：** heading; that outside block is a duplicate and must be deleted from every prompt. When the holder is offscreen, write only the visible remainder of a supported prop (the canopy stays in frame and the handle does not enter); do not write an empty handle or a floating umbrella. The start of a move is the volume the body occupies on this frame, including where the head, face, and chest point, not a compositional edge such as 下沿. If the previous facing is away from the person to be touched, write an in-place turn before the reach. The contacted sleeve, skin, or hand carries 图N, not 那只 or an unnamed 袖口和皮肤. 将扣、即将、and 接触尚未发生 are completed as a grab. State the result of a small move (很小的一步，迈完就停) rather than only 半步. 浅灰、深灰、亮明、阴暗 are exposure on whoever the light hits, not fog and not clothing or prop color; when gray light falls on a person, say not to fog the frame and not to change the clothes. Handbook: https://vrfi1sk8a0.feishu.cn/wiki/FIWjwgL33ipnkekzk30crmKUnIh
- For Seedance, H3, or other short-video work, agree the thesis and story structure with the user before writing prompts; do not jump to prompt output. The thesis should be simple enough for a market newcomer and usable as a short-video spine.
- Educational video should teach through a lived event (事教): keep the register serious, and land panic, fear, and the argument in the body through inner state and body-language close-ups, not by hearing a definition or diagramming structure first. Do not use props that stand in for rules. Split distinct themes into separate pieces rather than stuffing both into one film. Lecture- or voiceover-first structures are wrong.
- Project skills stay as a single copy under `.agents/skills/` (workspace root, and `Books Distillation/.agents/skills/` for vault workflow). Do not duplicate them into `.cursor/skills/`. `.cursor/` keeps rules, hooks, and MCP config.
