## Learned User Preferences

- Knowledge should enter and leave this vault through AI agents; stored notes still need meaningful structure and graph links, not an unstructured dump.
- Notes, briefings, and any organized explanation to the user should sound like a mentor: fluent, readable prose with complete sentences; no telegram-style fragments, keyword dumps, or generic AI cadence. Lead with judgment, then reasons; mark tradeoffs and failure modes without lecture.
- Prefer discuss-then-ingest: explore and challenge an idea first, then select, refine, and write only the durable remainder after the user agrees to save.
- When walking through a named book, stay inside that source: teach the core arguments and what is worth applying thoroughly enough that the user can work from the briefing as if they had read it. Treat the user as an experienced market participant who wants sharper judgment, not as a beginner. Do not pad with outside training knowledge unless asked to compare.
- When absorbing books or other sources, treat them as `source` notes plus atomic `concept` notes (short excerpts and processed understanding), not a wholesale paste of the original.
- Vault output is Chinese regardless of source language. For English sources, Chinese is not a sentence-level translation: understand the meaning first, then write as a native Chinese author—Chinese grammar, rhythm, and reading habits. Keep English only for terms, necessary quotations, or names without a stable Chinese rendering.
- Keep the vault scalable across books from multiple domains; avoid per-book knowledge silos. When a later book covers a similar idea, always落盘 the new angle—deepen the existing concept with multi-source corroboration if the mechanism matches, or create a linked concept if the mechanism differs; never skip because something “similar” already exists. Cross-era, cross-genre agreement raises a concept’s reference value.
- The user also edits the vault via the Obsidian Copilot plugin; Cursor and Copilot writes must stay consistent.
- Route video prompts by model. Seedance / 即梦 / 火山方舟 follow official Doubao Seedance 2.5 rules via the `seedance-prompt` skill; do not invent a prompt format. Thirty seconds is the per-shot ceiling, not the default length: set each shot by what the scene must do, keep openings short, and only let the peak approach 30 seconds; longer films are segmented or extended. Characters, locations, and hero props must be identity-locked across shots (stable appearance + reference binding; no face/wardrobe/set/prop swaps), given proper names, and those names must appear naturally in the scene—called, printed, or on a screen—not only as prompt IDs. In a frontal shot a phone shows only its back; cut to an over-shoulder or shoulder view to read the screen. Never put interface content on the phone back, and never show a face and screen text in the same frame. Canonical guide: https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/2607689?lang=zh
- MiniMax H3 / H3 Max / 海螺 / Hailuo / MiniMax Design prompts follow the `minimax-h3-prompt` skill: 4–15 second integer clips, product formula (duration + ratio + 图N binding + subject/scene/motion/sound), three modes (text, first/last frame, full reference). Do not reuse the Seedance timestamp template. Handbook: https://vrfi1sk8a0.feishu.cn/wiki/FIWjwgL33ipnkekzk30crmKUnIh
- For Seedance, H3, or other short-video work, agree the thesis and story structure with the user before writing prompts; do not jump to prompt output. The thesis should be simple enough for a market newcomer and usable as a short-video spine.
- Educational video should teach through a lived event (事教): keep the register serious, and land the argument through one strong pain or empathy beat the viewer feels in the body, not by hearing a definition. Lecture- or voiceover-first structures are wrong.
