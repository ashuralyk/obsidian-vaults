# Compact Development Example

This example demonstrates reasoning depth and artifact continuity. It is not a plot formula.

## Raw idea

A demolition engineer discovers people are still living inside a bridge scheduled to be destroyed at dawn.

The idea contains a visual situation and deadline, but not yet a personal conflict or ending.

## Qualified idea

On the night before a condemned bridge is demolished, a control-obsessed engineer must find and evacuate undocumented residents hidden inside its service chambers while her father, the contractor who falsified the clearance report, keeps the countdown running to save the family company.

Why cinema:

- a vast structure designed to fall;
- inaccessible chambers and unstable machinery;
- a visible countdown;
- technical actions that reveal character;
- a family argument expressed through competing uses of the bridge.

## Story engine

### Working title

《倒计时》

### Logline

When a demolition engineer discovers undocumented families still hiding inside a bridge wired to explode at dawn, she must evacuate them through the structure she designed to collapse while her father keeps the detonation on schedule to prevent their company—and his crime—from being exposed.

### Premise

The need to control every risk destroys trust; accepting shared responsibility makes rescue possible.

### Theme question

Is responsibility the power to prevent every failure, or the willingness to act openly when control has already failed?

### Genre contract

- Must deliver: structural danger, technical problem-solving, confined rescue, countdown, moral exposure.
- Exhausted device: arbitrary collapsing floors every few minutes.
- Distinction: each physical failure follows from an earlier compromise in the project and reveals family history.

## Ending-first design

### Event resolution

The residents leave through a maintenance span opened by a sequence of partial charges. The main detonation is canceled.

### Character ending

The engineer publicly signs the report that exposes both her father and her own earlier silence. She joins the residents outside the security perimeter instead of returning to the command trailer.

### Climactic choice

She gives charge control to the crew she has spent the film overruling, enters the bridge herself, and orders the destruction of the company's most valuable machinery to create the escape route.

### Opening and final images

- Opening: alone in a spotless control trailer, she corrects another engineer's hand placement before allowing him to touch the console.
- Final: covered in dust among residents and workers, she lets a junior engineer complete the shutdown while she signs the disclosure.

The visual contrast is not “dirty versus clean.” It proves a change from control without accountability to shared action with accountability.

## Protagonist engine

### Visible want

Complete the demolition without injury or delay.

### Unacknowledged need

Accept that responsibility includes exposing failure and relying on other people.

### Feared verdict

If anything escapes her control, she is as reckless as the people whose negligence killed her mother.

### Survival strategy

She anticipates every variable, withholds information until she has a solution, and takes over other people's tasks.

### Strength and flaw

- Strength: exceptional systems thinking under pressure.
- Flaw from the same source: she turns colleagues into passive instruments and conceals uncertainty.

### Boundary behavior

At the opening, she would never surrender control of the charges or reveal an unsolved danger.

## Opposition map

### Father

- Wants the demolition completed before an inspection window opens.
- Believes thousands of workers should not lose their livelihood for several residents who ignored evacuation orders.
- Can alter the countdown, security access, and public information.
- Cannot leave because he must authorize the blast and protect the company.

### Shared core

Father and daughter both believe responsibility means carrying danger alone and hiding uncertainty from others.

### Disowned possibility

She condemns his willingness to treat people as numbers, but does the same when she withholds information “until the system is stable.”

### Third answer

The climax rejects his concealment but retains his capacity for decisive technical action. She acts quickly while making the risk public and distributing agency.

## Relationship line

The secondary relationship is with a junior engineer whom the protagonist repeatedly overruns.

1. He admits he missed a sensor anomaly; she silently fixes it and excludes him.
2. She reveals that the clearance data may be false; he asks to alert the crew, and she refuses until she has proof.
3. Trapped inside the bridge, she admits she cannot calculate the charge sequence alone. He receives control and completes the computation.

His knowledge changes the final method. Removing him would force the climax back into solitary heroics, so the relationship line is structural.

## Causal spine

1. She prepares a flawless demolition.
2. A thermal sensor shows movement inside a cleared chamber.
3. Her father calls it an animal and refuses delay.
4. She enters the bridge secretly to verify the reading.
5. She finds a family; her unauthorized entry triggers a security lockdown.
6. At midpoint, she learns the residents are not an isolated family and that her own signature validated the false clearance report.
7. Her father accelerates the countdown while residents distrust her uniform and refuse to follow.
8. Her solo evacuation plan collapses; an injured resident and severed access route make total control impossible.
9. The junior engineer's local sensor knowledge and the residents' map of hidden chambers combine into a partial-charge escape plan.
10. She publicly cancels the blast, transfers control, and destroys company equipment to open the route.
11. The residents escape; the bridge remains standing but unusable.
12. She signs the disclosure and remains outside the old chain of command.

Each turn follows from an earlier choice: secrecy causes lockdown, prior silence makes her complicit, control creates distrust, and distrust destroys the solo plan.

## Example scene card

# Card 18: The signature

- Location / time: submerged inspection chamber, midnight.
- Present characters: engineer, resident organizer.
- Point of view: engineer.
- Immediate objective: persuade residents to follow her evacuation route.
- Obstacle: organizer believes she represents the company that erased them.
- First tactic: establish technical authority.
- Counteraction: organizer produces the clearance sheet bearing her signature.
- Tactic change: she claims the underlying survey was falsified.
- Turn: organizer reveals that she was warned about missing households months earlier.
- Before → after value: rescuer with authority → implicated official without trust.
- Plot and character work: blocks the easy evacuation and exposes her defensive habit of signing off before confronting uncertainty.
- Why the next card follows: she must choose whether to inform the command trailer and risk cancellation.

## Scene specification

### Purpose

Remove the protagonist's moral authority and make trust—not engineering—the immediate obstacle.

### Entry

Enter as she finishes marking the evacuation route. Do not show the entire search.

### Action/reaction ladder

1. She orders the residents to gather.
2. The organizer asks who certified the empty bridge.
3. She changes from command to technical explanation.
4. The organizer hands her the signed report.
5. She changes from explanation to blame.
6. The organizer reveals the earlier warning.

### Physical carrier

Water rises across the printed route while they argue. The map remains readable long enough to matter; the water is consequence, not decorative suspense.

### Exposition in play

The signature, survey, and warning enter as evidence in a struggle over who should be trusted.

### Exit

Cut when she opens her radio to report the residents, then closes it without speaking.

### Before → after

She enters believing information will restore authority and leaves knowing that disclosure will implicate her.

## Dialogue beat sketch

The dialogue should not begin with an explanation of the falsified survey.

1. She commands; organizer refuses.
2. She invokes expertise; organizer presents the signature.
3. She blames corrupted data; organizer reveals the warning.
4. She asks for time; organizer uses her own countdown against her.

The unsaid contest is not “Is the route safe?” It is “Will you risk yourself before asking us to trust you?”

## Rewrite diagnosis example

Suppose a draft reader says the midpoint feels flat. Do not add an explosion first.

Diagnosis:

- The thermal discovery increases quantity but not meaning.
- The protagonist still sees herself as an innocent rescuer.
- Pressure remains technical.

Repair:

- Move the signed clearance report to the midpoint.
- Make the protagonist's prior avoidance part of the current danger.
- Change the second half from “engineer versus structure” to “complicit engineer must earn cooperation while solving the structure.”

Success condition:

After the midpoint, every technical decision also changes trust and exposure.
