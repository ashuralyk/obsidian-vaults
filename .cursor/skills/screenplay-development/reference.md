# Screenplay Development Reference

## What each framework contributes

Use each framework for its own question. Do not merge their terminology into one formula.

### Syd Field: the line must be traversable

Use for:

- setup, confrontation, and resolution;
- two large directional turns;
- designing backward from the ending;
- carding scenes before drafting;
- completing the first draft.

Do not treat approximate page positions as proof that the story works. A plot point changes direction; it is not synonymous with every later beat system.

### Richard Walter: every retained element must earn its place

Use for:

- beginning late and ending early;
- testing whether detail advances plot and reveals character;
- writing visible and audible material;
- preserving discovery after outlining;
- finishing before polishing;
- cutting attractive material that blocks the story.

Theme may emerge during writing. Do not force a premature slogan onto discoveries that contradict it.

### Robert McKee on dialogue: speech is action

Use for:

- scene objectives;
- verbal tactics and counteractions;
- beats created by strategy changes;
- exposition inside conflict;
- subtext and indirect expression;
- character-specific vocabulary and syntax.

Naturalistic talk is not automatically dramatic. Dialogue can sound casual while remaining concentrated on action and consequence.

### Lajos Egri: premise, character, conflict

Use for:

- a causal premise that includes character force, conflict, and result;
- physiological, social, and psychological character causes;
- plot generated by choice;
- opposition that is incompatible yet bound together;
- escalating action and counteraction.

Egri's premise is not a logline or an open theme question. His “orchestrating character” is a conflict initiator, not necessarily the protagonist.

### Julian Hoxter: development and revision workflow

Use for:

- theme expressed through changing attitudes;
- archetypes as relationship functions;
- logline, outline, and treatment as different documents;
- secondary stories that return to the main line;
- pace as allocation of attention;
- rewriting one problem across the whole draft.

His workflow is not a fixed beat sheet.

### Blake Snyder: commercial pressure testing

Use for:

- testing the logline with real listeners;
- identifying genre promises and exhausted solutions;
- selecting the protagonist under maximum conflict;
- requiring an active choice into the second movement;
- changing pressure at the midpoint;
- invalidating the old solution;
- comparing opening and final images;
- diagnosing structure with cards and scene value changes.

Fifteen beats and roughly forty cards are diagnostic scales, not natural laws.

### William Indick: psychological hypotheses become action

Use for:

- defense mechanisms expressed in behavior;
- compensation hiding a feared verdict;
- strength and flaw emerging from one survival strategy;
- an antagonist carrying a disowned possibility;
- intimacy earned through vulnerable action;
- external goals concentrating existential anxiety;
- separating event resolution from character ending.

Freudian stages, collective-unconscious claims, birth-order determinism, and gender-essentialist journeys are not modern psychological facts. Keep only hypotheses that produce testable choices and consequences.

## Document distinctions

### Idea

A generative possibility. It may not yet contain a protagonist or action.

### Subject

The person and the activity: “this person doing this thing.”

### Logline

A communication and development test:

> When [disruption], a [distinct protagonist] must [active goal] against [opposition], or [stakes], in a situation defined by [contrast].

Do not add lore until the engine becomes understandable. Proper names rarely help at this stage.

### Premise

A causal claim:

> [Character force] under [conflict] leads to [result].

It selects material but need not be universally true.

### Theme question

An arguable question different characters answer through action. The protagonist's ending supplies the movie's earned answer.

### Synopsis

A prose account of the complete story, including the ending.

### Outline

A navigational record of dramatic units and their functions. Update it when structural discoveries change the route.

### Treatment

A readable presentation of the movie's flow and tone for another reader. It is not a screenplay and should not be mistaken for one.

## Full development diagnostics

### Idea and cinematic potential

- Is there a person doing something, not merely a situation?
- Can the central pressure generate two hours of consequential action?
- Does the idea promise behavior, place, sound, and images?
- Is the unique appeal present in the story rather than only the pitch?
- Why must events begin now?

### Logline

- Is the protagonist specific without relying on a proper name?
- Is the goal active and visible?
- Can the opposition keep acting?
- Does failure cost something personal?
- Is there a clear contrast, irony, or unusual pressure?
- Does the sentence hide a vague second act?

### Premise and theme

- Does the premise state a causal result rather than a topic?
- Can the opening character plausibly contain the ending?
- Do major events test the same causal claim?
- Do supporting characters embody competing answers?
- Is the climax an action that answers the theme question?

### Protagonist

- Why this character and not another person in the same world?
- What do they want now?
- What do they fear the outcome will prove about them?
- What strategy once protected them?
- How is that strategy both strength and flaw?
- What would they never voluntarily do at the beginning?
- Why must they do it at the climax?
- Which major turn is still caused by someone else?

### Antagonist

- What does the antagonist want independently?
- Why is that desire reasonable from their position?
- How can they alter the situation?
- Why can neither side leave?
- What do both sides share?
- What does the protagonist condemn in the antagonist but practice secretly?
- If the antagonist vanished, would the protagonist still need to change?

### Relationship system

- What function does each major character perform now?
- Does that function change?
- Which relationship carries the theme into lived action?
- What vulnerability is disclosed, at what risk, and how is it answered?
- What does the secondary line teach or train that the climax requires?
- Would the final solution remain unchanged if the secondary line were removed?

### Causal structure

- What is stable at the opening?
- What disrupts it?
- What active choice commits the protagonist?
- What counteraction follows each action?
- How does the midpoint change deadline, exposure, stakes, relationship, or meaning?
- What internal fracture grows with external pressure?
- Which old strategy fails at the lowest point?
- Where does the new solution come from?
- Why can only this protagonist make the climactic choice?
- What event is resolved?
- What identity or way of living is resolved?

### Scene cards

- Does the card contain an action rather than a mood?
- Who owns the scene?
- What does that person want before leaving?
- Who or what blocks it?
- What tactic is attempted?
- How does the other side respond?
- What changes before the cut?
- Why must the next card follow?
- Which story line receives development?
- Are adjacent cards doing the same work?

### Scene execution

- Can the entrance occur later?
- Is conflict active immediately?
- Does physical behavior carry part of the meaning?
- Is the setting exerting pressure?
- Does exposition serve a character's objective?
- Does someone change strategy?
- Is there a genuine turn?
- Can the exit occur earlier?
- Does the scene both advance plot and expose character?
- Is its emotional direction too similar to surrounding scenes?

### Dialogue

- What action does each line perform on a listener?
- What result does the speaker expect?
- What response defeats the current tactic?
- Where does the tactic change?
- What cannot be said directly?
- Can a third object carry the contested meaning?
- Does the vocabulary arise from the character's life?
- Is the line duplicated by image, action, or another line?
- Would silence be stronger?
- Does reading aloud expose padding or written syntax?

### Page craft

- Is every statement visible, audible, or performable?
- Does each action sentence have a clear subject?
- Are paragraphs short enough to suggest screen rhythm?
- Has the writer avoided directing routine coverage?
- Is backstory excluded unless it affects present action?
- Are character introductions selective and playable?
- Are scene headings, names, and continuity consistent?

### First draft

- Is there a protected writing schedule?
- Is the draft moving forward rather than restarting?
- Are provisional scenes allowed?
- Has a discovery invalidated the current outline?
- If so, was the route updated instead of forcing the old plan?
- Has resistance disguised itself as research, formatting, or a new idea?

### Rewrite

- What is the single objective of this pass?
- Which scenes contain the problem?
- Is the apparent dialogue problem actually structural?
- Will fixing an early choice alter later causality?
- Has the new version created a contradiction elsewhere?
- Is the best-written scene preventing the correct story?

## Failure patterns and remedies

### Strong concept, inert screenplay

Likely causes:

- wrong protagonist;
- passive goal;
- weak antagonist;
- external events not caused by choice.

Repair the protagonist and causal spine before adding set pieces.

### Good first act, empty middle

Likely causes:

- opposition can be escaped;
- actions do not trigger counteractions;
- midpoint repeats rather than changes pressure;
- secondary line does not affect the main line.

Strengthen binding relationships and redesign consequences.

### Busy plot, shallow character

Likely causes:

- strength and flaw are unrelated;
- psychological language never becomes behavior;
- losses do not attack the survival strategy;
- climax requires skill but not change.

Trace one defense from early success through mounting cost to final choice.

### Interesting characters, no movie

Likely causes:

- wants remain private or abstract;
- relationships contain no incompatible outcomes;
- scenes reveal but do not alter;
- no ending was chosen.

Give inner pressure an external goal and build backward from a consequential ending.

### Expository dialogue

Likely causes:

- characters tell each other what they know;
- information has no owner or tactical use;
- background arrives before the audience needs it.

Delay information and make it a resource in conflict.

### Formulaic structure

Likely causes:

- beat labels substituted for causality;
- cards were filled to quota;
- protagonist behavior exists to reach page targets.

Remove labels and retell the story only as choices and consequences.

### Flat ending

Likely causes:

- event resolved but identity did not;
- old solution succeeded with more force;
- final image announces change without earned behavior.

Separate event resolution from character ending and redesign the climactic choice.

## Professional handoff package

For a developed feature project, deliver:

1. Title and one-sentence promise.
2. Logline.
3. Premise and theme question.
4. Genre contract and comparison analysis.
5. Protagonist character engine.
6. Antagonist and relationship map.
7. One-page causal spine.
8. Beat sheet with functions, not only labels.
9. Card outline.
10. Character and location continuity sheet.
11. Screenplay draft.
12. Revision memo listing passes completed and unresolved risks.

Do not claim the package is production-ready solely because every document exists. The screenplay itself remains the authority.
